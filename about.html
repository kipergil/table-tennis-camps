About Table Tennis Camps

Table Tennis Camps is one place to see upcoming table tennis camps around the world, for every age and level, without following dozens of club websites, federation calendars and booking pages. It's free, there's no account, and the list is refreshed every Friday.

Every listing links to a public page from the organiser or a ticket site. Nothing is invented: if a detail such as the price or the start time isn't published, the field is simply left out.

Types of camp

Each camp gets one colour, used everywhere on the site: the filter chips, the bar beside each listing, the calendar and the map pins.

  • Holiday campDay camps in the school holidays, mostly for children and juniors, often open to beginners.
  • Adult & club campMulti-day training for adult and club players, usually from local-league standard upwards.
  • High-performance campIntensive or selective training for competitive players: pathway, squad, pro and pre-season camps.
  • Residential campCamps and summer schools where you stay over, or travel for a training week.
  • Skills clinicShort, themed sessions, often one day, on a single skill such as serves, footwork or multi-ball.

How to use it

  • Search looks through titles, descriptions, venues, towns, countries and organisers. Press the ✕ or Esc to clear it.
  • List, Calendar and Map switch between views. Your filters carry across all three.
  • Date filters: This week, Next week, This month, Next month and Later. A camp that runs over several days counts on every day it runs, so a winter camp that started yesterday still shows under This week. Press a selected filter again to clear it.
  • Type filters show one kind of camp. The number on each chip is how many camps you'd see with that choice, together with your other filters.
  • Where: pick a country to see only its camps, then a city from the row of city chips that appears under it. In menu mode, one "Where" menu lists every country with its cities. Camps whose organiser doesn't give a town are still counted under their country.
  • Pills or menus: "Use menus" swaps the chips for two drop-down menus, which some people find easier with a keyboard or screen reader. Your choice is remembered in this browser.
  • The boxes in the header marked "on this week" and "on this month" apply that date filter when you press them.
  • Share a view: every filter is saved in the page address, so you can copy the link to send exactly what you're looking at.
  • On each listing, the title and the View button open the organiser's page. The venue opens Google Maps. "Add to calendar" opens a pre-filled Google Calendar event. "Venue on map" shows the camp on this site's map.
  • Calendar: weeks start on Monday. Select a day to list everything on it. When a date filter is set, its days are outlined and the rest are dimmed.
  • Map: one pin per venue, coloured by the most common type of camp there. Numbers on clusters count camps, not venues. A camp only gets a pin when its exact venue could be confirmed.
  • Already running: longer camps that have already started are grouped at the end of the list.

Check before you go

Listings are gathered automatically from public pages and checked by hand where they can't be. Camps sell out, move or change price, and some organisers only publish part of the details. Always confirm dates, times, prices, age limits and the level required on the organiser's page before you book or travel. Times are local to the venue.

Something missing or wrong?

If you run a camp or know of one that isn't here, the quickest way to get it listed is to make sure it has a public page with the dates (including the year), the venue address and a way to book, such as a club website, a federation calendar or a ticket site. Pages like that are picked up during the weekly check, every Friday. If a listing is wrong, the organiser's page is always the one to trust, and corrections there will appear here after the next Friday refresh.
